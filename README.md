# CYBERSECURITY FUNDAMENTALS

## Understanding Threats, Vulnerabilities and Risks

These three concepts are essential in cybersecurity risk assessment. Although they are closely related, they have different meanings.

### Threat

A threat is a potential danger that could exploit a vulnerability and cause harm to a system, network, or organization.

Threats can originate from malicious individuals, cybercriminals, malware, or even accidental human actions.

**Examples:**
- Malware infections.
- Phishing attacks.
- Ransomware attacks.
- Unauthorized access attempts.
- Insider threats.

### Vulnerability

A vulnerability is a weakness or flaw in a system, application, network, or security process that could be exploited by a threat.

Vulnerabilities create opportunities for attackers to compromise systems.

**Examples:**
- Weak passwords.
- Unpatched software.
- Misconfigured systems.
- Unnecessary open ports.
- Inadequate access controls.

### Risk

Risk is the potential for loss or damage when a threat exploits a vulnerability.

In cybersecurity, risk assessment considers the likelihood of an incident occurring and the potential impact if it happens.

A simplified way to understand risk is:

**Risk = Likelihood × Impact**

The higher the likelihood and potential impact of an incident, the greater the associated risk.

## Understanding the Relationship Between Threats, Vulnerabilities and Risks

Consider this scenario:

An organization uses weak passwords to protect employee accounts.

- **Vulnerability:** Weak password security.
- **Threat:** A cybercriminal attempting to gain unauthorized access.
- **Threat event:** The attacker successfully guesses or obtains an employee's password.
- **Impact:** Unauthorized access to company accounts and possible exposure of sensitive information.
- **Risk:** The organization could experience data exposure, financial loss, and reputational damage.

Understanding the relationship between these concepts helps cybersecurity professionals identify weaknesses and recommend appropriate security controls.

## How to Write Cybersecurity Risk Statements

A risk statement clearly describes what could happen, why it could happen, and the potential consequences.

A useful risk statement should identify:

1. The vulnerability or weakness.
2. The threat event that could exploit it.
3. The potential impact.

### Risk Statement Formula

**Because of [vulnerability/weakness], [threat event] could occur, resulting in [potential impact].**

### Example 1: Weak Passwords

| Element | Description |
|---|---|
| Vulnerability | Weak password policy |
| Threat | Cybercriminal |
| Threat event | Unauthorized access to employee accounts |
| Impact | Sensitive information exposure |

**Risk Statement:**

Because of a weak password policy, a cybercriminal could gain unauthorized access to company accounts, potentially exposing sensitive information.

### Example 2: Unpatched Software

| Element | Description |
|---|---|
| Vulnerability | Unpatched software |
| Threat | Cyberattacker |
| Threat event | Exploitation of a known software vulnerability |
| Impact | System compromise |

**Risk Statement:**

Because the organization's software is not regularly patched, a cyberattacker could exploit a known vulnerability and compromise the system, potentially resulting in data loss or unauthorized access.

### Why Risk Statements Matter

Risk statements help organizations:

- Identify security weaknesses.
- Understand potential attack scenarios.
- Evaluate the consequences of security incidents.
- Prioritize security improvements.
- Communicate cybersecurity risks to management.
- Make informed security decisions.
